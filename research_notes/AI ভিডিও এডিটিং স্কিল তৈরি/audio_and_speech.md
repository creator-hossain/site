Research date: 2026-10-28. Scope: Bengali (Bangladesh, bn-BD) Reels/TikTok/Shorts. ## 1. Bengali ASR with accurate WORD-level timestamps (open models and cloud APIs, WER, price)

### Takeaway
Stock Whisper (large-v3 and especially large-v3-turbo) is weak on Bengali: about 30-37% CER zero-shot, and roughly 70-79% WER on two independent test sets. Use a Bengali fine-tuned model instead. Good options are an open fine-tune (Whisper-medium "tugstugi/BengaliAI", Mozilla's large-v3-turbo-bn, AI4Bharat IndicWhisper, or the MIT-licensed IndicConformer), or a commercial API that lists Bengali (ElevenLabs Scribe, Deepgram Nova-3, Google Chirp 3 bn-BD in Preview, AssemblyAI Universal, AWS bn-IN batch). Then get word timings from the model's own timestamps or from a separate alignment pass (Section 2). WhisperX has NO default Bengali alignment model.

### Cited Findings

**Stock Whisper on Bengali (zero-shot)**
- The BuzzASR Bengali model card reports zero-shot Whisper large-v3 **CER 30.46 on FLEURS-bn, 37.31 on Common Voice 25, and 34.04 combined**. The fine-tuned monolingual BuzzASR-bn reaches **WER 31.97 (FLEURS) / 19.42 (CV25)**, described as about a 4.4x CER reduction over Whisper. — [BuzzASR/bengali model card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/bengali); paper: [BuzzASR: A Swarm of 100+ Monolingual Speech Recognition Models (arXiv 2609.09554, Sept 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09554)
- The Dolphin paper (Table 6, a proprietary Dataocean AI test set, not FLEURS/CV) gives Whisper Bengali WER of **base 115.7 / small 136.1 / medium 127.0 / large-v3 71.0**. — [Dolphin, arXiv 2503.20212](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20212)
- On 9,363 Bengali Common Voice samples, Mozilla.ai's model card scores the unmodified **Whisper large-v3-turbo baseline at a normalized WER of 78.843**. Fine-tuning on about 21,400 CV samples lowers it to **11.053**. — [mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-turbo-bn (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-turbo-bn)
- A 2025 comparative study (Whisper Small/Large-V2 vs Wav2Vec-BERT on Bangla) found Whisper had higher error rates on Bangla and was much more resource-intensive. — [arXiv 2507.01931](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01931v1)
- A Sony Research India paper notes that Whisper struggles on low-resource Indian languages and decodes them more slowly, because its tokenizer has fewer tokens for them. — [arXiv 2412.19785](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19785v1)

**Bengali fine-tuned open models**
- **bangla-speech-processing/BanglaASR**: Whisper-small (about 244M params) fine-tuned on Common Voice Bangla (about 40k train / 7k val clips). Its card reports **WER 4.58%** on its own split. This is self-reported and not comparable to other benchmarks. — [HF card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/bangla-speech-processing/BanglaASR)
- **hassanaliemon/BanglaASR**: Whisper-small trained on 23.8 h, WER 14.73. — [HF card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/hassanaliemon/BanglaASR)
- A 2026 long-form Bengali study ran WhisperX with anuragshas/whisper-large-v2-bn, tugstugi_bengaliai-asr_whisper-medium, tanmoyio/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-bengali and ai4bharat/indicwav2vec_v1_bengali. It found **tugstugi_bengaliai-asr_whisper-medium consistently gave the lowest WER** and reports WER 0.38 on the (DL Sprint 4.0 Kaggle) private leaderboard for long-form, noisy audio. — [arXiv 2603.03158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03158)
- A second team on the same long-form task reached **private WER 0.37738 / public 0.36137**, using **Demucs source separation**, silence-boundary chunking and tuned generation settings. — [arXiv 2602.21741](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21741v1)
- Bangla-WhisperDiar (May 2026) fine-tunes tugstugi's Bengali regional Whisper-medium plus PyAnnote and reports WER 0.2441 on its long-form test set. — [arXiv 2605.08214](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.08214v1)
- **AI4Bharat IndicWhisper (Vistaar)** Bengali WER: **Kathbath 16.6, Kathbath-Hard 19.4, CommonVoice 24.7, FLEURS 20.9, IndicTTS 18.8 (avg 20.1)**. The repo is "MIT-licensed", and the license "applies to all the fine-tuned language models". The Bengali checkpoint is a zip at `indicwhisper.objectstore.e2enetworks.net/bengali_models.zip`. — [AI4Bharat/vistaar GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/vistaar)
- **AI4Bharat IndicConformer** (hybrid CTC-RNNT Conformer) has a Bengali model `ai4bharat/indicconformer_stt_bn_hybrid_ctc_rnnt_large` under the **MIT** license. The multilingual `indic-conformer-600m-multilingual` is also MIT but gated (requires sharing contact info). — [HF card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indicconformer_stt_bn_hybrid_ctc_rnnt_large/raw/main/README.md); [AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR); [600M multilingual](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indic-conformer-600m-multilingual)
- **Meta MMS mms-1b-all**: fine-tuned on 1162 languages, license **cc-by-nc-4.0** (non-commercial). A community Bengali fine-tune (Umong/wav2vec2-large-mms-1b-bengali, CV13) reports WER about 0.33 and is also CC-BY-NC-4.0. — [facebook/mms-1b-all (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all); [Umong/wav2vec2-large-mms-1b-bengali](https://huggingface.co/Umong/wav2vec2-large-mms-1b-bengali/blob/main/README.md)

**Word-timestamp tooling on top of Whisper-family models**
- **WhisperX alignment**: the `DEFAULT_ALIGN_MODELS_TORCH` dict covers en/fr/de/es/it, and `DEFAULT_ALIGN_MODELS_HF` covers about 36 languages, including **hi, ur, te, ml but NOT bn**. For bn with no `model_name`, WhisperX raises `ValueError("No default align-model for language: ...")`. Any HF wav2vec2 CTC model can be passed via `--align_model` / `model_name`. — [whisperX/whisperx/alignment.py](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/blob/main/whisperx/alignment.py)
- Candidate Bengali wav2vec2 CTC checkpoints to use as the WhisperX `--align_model`: `tanmoyio/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-bengali` (XLSR-53 on OpenSLR; card reports test WER 88.58; 16 kHz input), `ai4bharat/indicwav2vec_v1_bengali`, `Tahsin-Mayeesha/wav2vec2-bn-300m`, `SayedShaun/bangla-wave2vec2` (weights mirrored from Kaggle, so provenance is unclear). — [tanmoyio card](https://huggingface.co/tanmoyio/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-bengali/blame/refs%2Fpr%2F1/README.md); [Spark NLP page for Tahsin-Mayeesha model](https://sparknlp.org/2025/04/01/wav2vec2_bengali_300m_pipeline_bn.html); [SayedShaun/bangla-wave2vec2](https://huggingface.co/SayedShaun/bangla-wave2vec2); [arXiv 2603.03158](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03158)
- **stable-ts** (MIT): word timestamps from Whisper cross-attention + DTW (`word_timestamps=True` by default). `refine()` mutes audio portions to tighten word start/end (default precision 0.1 s). `align()` aligns a known plain-text transcript (requires `language`). Backends: openai-whisper, faster-whisper (`stable-ts[fw]`, `load_faster_whisper()`), HF Transformers (`stable-ts[hf]`, `load_hf_whisper()`). Exports SRT/VTT/ASS/TSV/JSON with word-level highlighting, including an **ASS `karaoke` option**. The README does not list per-language support or Bengali accuracy. — [jianfch/stable-ts](https://github.com/jianfch/stable-ts)
- **whisper-timestamped**: also DTW on cross-attention, but licensed **AGPL-3.0** (dependency dtw-python is GPLv3). Backends are `openai-whisper` and `transformers` (no faster-whisper mention). VAD options: silero (default; the README warns newer silero versions can false-alarm) and auditok. It can load fine-tuned HF Whisper models. — [linto-ai/whisper-timestamped](https://github.com/linto-ai/whisper-timestamped)

### Inferences
- **Recommended self-hosted stack for Bengali captions:** (1) Demucs/BS-RoFormer vocal isolation if there is background music, (2) VAD chunking, (3) a Bengali fine-tuned Whisper (tugstugi BengaliAI whisper-medium, or mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-turbo-bn after licence check) run through faster-whisper/CTranslate2 or stable-ts, (4) re-time words with a CTC forced aligner (Section 2) against the corrected transcript. The two 2026 Kaggle write-ups both used separation + chunking + a fine-tuned Whisper-medium, which supports this design.
- Whisper cross-attention DTW timestamps (stable-ts, whisper-timestamped, faster-whisper `word_timestamps=True`) are language-agnostic but tend to drift by about 100-300 ms on weak languages. A CTC alignment pass is the safer route to frame-accurate word pops. This is an engineering judgement, not a measured Bengali figure.
- whisper-timestamped's AGPL-3.0 licence matters if the pipeline is offered as a network service. stable-ts (MIT) or WhisperX (licence not verified here) are lower-risk.
- For a "fast and cheap" cloud fallback with word timestamps: ElevenLabs Scribe (about $0.22-0.40/h) or Deepgram Nova-3 (about $0.26/h batch) are the cheapest options listing Bengali. Google Chirp 3 bn-BD is Preview and warns of degraded accuracy with word timestamps on.
- A Bengali caption pipeline should normalise output (Bengali digits vs Latin digits, the dari "।", ZWJ/ZWNJ in conjuncts) before alignment and rendering, because romanizers and CTC vocabularies differ on these.

### Gaps
- No clean, apples-to-apples Bengali WER table for large-v3 vs large-v3-turbo vs cloud APIs on the same test set (e.g., FLEURS-bn) was found. The Whisper paper appendix tables (FLEURS per-language) were not accessible.
- Licences of tugstugi/BengaliAI whisper-medium, anuragshas/whisper-large-v2-bn, mozilla-ai/whisper-large-v3-turbo-bn and BuzzASR-bn were not verified (HF unreachable). Check each model card before commercial use. WhisperX's own licence (believed BSD-2-Clause) and faster-whisper's (believed MIT) were not verified in this session.
- No bn-BD vs bn-IN dialect accuracy data for any cloud API. AWS only offers bn-IN.
- Azure bn-BD/bn-IN STT support and all Azure/AssemblyAI per-minute prices were not confirmed (pages unreachable).
- Accuracy of Whisper DTW word timestamps on Bengali (ms error) is unmeasured in any source found.

## 2. Forced alignment when the transcript/SRT is already known (Bengali)

### Takeaway
For Bengali, the practical aligners are MMS-based CTC alignment (ctc-forced-aligner, or torchaudio's MMS_FA pipeline with romanization) and WhisperX/ctc-forced-aligner with a Bengali wav2vec2 CTC model. However, the default MMS aligner weights are **CC-BY-NC** (non-commercial), and **torchaudio's `forced_align` API was deprecated in 2.8 and removed in 2.9**. A commercial pipeline should therefore pair a permissively licensed Bengali CTC model (e.g., MIT IndicConformer CTC head, or a checked wav2vec2-bn) with a standalone CTC Viterbi aligner, or pin torchaudio ≤2.8.

### Cited Findings
- **ctc-forced-aligner (MahmoudAshraf97)**: code is **BSD-2-Clause**. The default model `MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner` is **CC-BY-NC 4.0**, and the README recommends a different model for commercial work. It supports "1130" languages via ISO 639-3 codes (Bengali = `ben`). `--romanize` is needed for non-Latin scripts with the default model. Output is JSON `segments` with `start`/`end` (seconds, 3 decimals) and `text`. The default `--split_size` is word (sentence/char also available). It claims "at least 5X less memory" than the TorchAudio API and runs on CUDA or CPU. CLI: `ctc-forced-aligner --audio_path a.wav --text_path t.txt --language "ben" --romanize`. Other CTC models with a native vocabulary (e.g., a wav2vec2 model) can be swapped in via `--alignment_model`. — [ctc-forced-aligner GitHub](https://github.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner)
- **torchaudio forced alignment**: `torchaudio.functional.forced_align()` (with CPU/CUDA kernels, developed alongside MMS) and its multilingual MMS_FA tutorial were **deprecated in torchaudio 2.8 and removed in 2.9**, as part of torchaudio's maintenance-phase refactor (tracking issue pytorch/audio #3902). — [torchaudio 2.9 CTC alignment tutorial (via search snippet)](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.9.0/tutorials/ctc_forced_alignment_api_tutorial.html); [torchaudio 2.8 multilingual tutorial](https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/2.8/tutorials/forced_alignment_for_multilingual_data_tutorial.html)
- **WhisperX alignment with a custom model**: pass any HF wav2vec2 CTC checkpoint via `--align_model`. The language code still controls sentence splitting (falls back to English Punkt if unmapped) and whether words are space-separated (only ja/zh are treated as non-spaced). — [whisperX alignment.py](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX/blob/main/whisperx/alignment.py)
- **stable-ts `align()`** aligns a known plain-text transcript to audio using Whisper itself (no CTC model needed), so it can use any Whisper checkpoint including a Bengali fine-tune. It is MIT licensed. — [stable-ts](https://github.com/jianfch/stable-ts)
- IndicConformer models are hybrid **CTC**-RNNT and MIT-licensed, so their CTC head can supply emissions for Viterbi forced alignment in a commercial pipeline. — [IndicConformer bn card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/ai4bharat/indicconformer_stt_bn_hybrid_ctc_rnnt_large/raw/main/README.md); [IndicConformerASR GitHub](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/IndicConformerASR)

### Inferences
- **Best quality-per-effort for Bengali captions:** produce the transcript (ASR + optional human or LLM correction), then run ctc-forced-aligner with `--language ben --romanize` for prototyping. For commercial production, swap `--alignment_model` to a permissively licensed Bengali CTC model and drop romanization if the model's vocab is Bengali script.
- If using torchaudio's MMS_FA bundle directly, pin `torchaudio==2.8.*` (where it still exists, deprecated) or vendor the CTC Viterbi code. Do not build on torchaudio ≥2.9 for alignment.
- Montreal Forced Aligner, aeneas and the NeMo Forced Aligner were not researched in depth. NeMo NFA would pair naturally with IndicConformer (a NeMo model), which makes it a plausible all-permissive Bengali route.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether Montreal Forced Aligner has a pretrained Bengali acoustic model or dictionary; aeneas Bengali support (it uses TTS-based DTW, so it depends on eSpeak's Bengali voice); the NeMo Forced Aligner licence and IndicConformer compatibility.
- No measured word-boundary accuracy (ms) for Bengali with any aligner was found.
- Licences of the candidate Bengali wav2vec2 CTC models (tanmoyio, Tahsin-Mayeesha, indicwav2vec) were not verified. indicwav2vec's licence is unknown here.

## 3. Analysing a reference video's sound design (separation, onsets, tagging, beats)

### Takeaway
Separate voice from bed with BS-RoFormer (via `audio-separator`, MIT; vocal SDR about 12.9 dB) or HT-Demucs (MIT, but archived since Jan 2025). Then run onset detection on the non-vocal stem and tag each onset window with PANNs (MIT code, 527 AudioSet classes, frame-wise SED) and/or LAION-CLAP (CC0 repo) to classify SFX types (whoosh, pop, riser, ding). Watch licences: madmom's pretrained models are CC BY-NC-SA and Essentia is AGPL. beat_this (MIT, secondary source) is a safer beat tracker for commercial use.

### Cited Findings
- **python-audio-separator** (MIT; asks for credit to UVR) wraps MDX-Net (.onnx), VR, Demucs (.yaml) and MDXC/**BS-RoFormer / Mel-RoFormer** (.ckpt). The top vocal model `model_bs_roformer_ep_317_sdr_12.9755.ckpt` scores **vocals 12.9 dB / instrumental 17.0 dB SDR**, and `htdemucs_ft` scores vocals 10.8. CLI: `audio-separator in.wav --model_filename model_bs_roformer_ep_317_sdr_12.9755.ckpt`. Install with `pip install "audio-separator[gpu]"` (CUDA 11.8/12.2) or `[cpu]`. Docker images exist for amd64/arm64. No separate weight licence is stated. — [nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator](https://github.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator)
- **Demucs**: MIT. The repo was **archived on 1 Jan 2025** ("not maintained anymore"), and only important bug fixes go to the fork `adefossez/demucs`. HT-Demucs scores 9.00 dB SDR on MUSDB-HQ. `htdemucs_ft` is about 4x slower and slightly better, and `htdemucs_6s` adds guitar/piano (piano weak). `demucs --two-stems=vocals file.mp3` gives no speed benefit because it still separates all stems. — [facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)
- Demucs separation before ASR was used by a top Bengali long-form ASR team (private WER 0.377). — [arXiv 2602.21741](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21741v1)
- **PANNs (audioset_tagging_cnn)**: code is MIT. CNN14 reaches mAP 0.431 (16 kHz variant 0.438), and Wavegram-Logmel-CNN 0.439, across **527 AudioSet classes**. Frame-wise **sound event detection** works via DecisionLevelMax (mAP 0.385). Usage: `pytorch/inference.py audio_tagging|sound_event_detection --model_type=Cnn14 --checkpoint_path=... --cuda`. Checkpoints are on Zenodo, and their licence is not stated on the page. — [qiuqiangkong/audioset_tagging_cnn](https://github.com/qiuqiangkong/audioset_tagging_cnn)
- **LAION-CLAP**: the repo is **CC0-1.0**. Checkpoints: `630k-audioset-best.pt` (general audio <10 s), `630k-audioset-fusion-best.pt` (variable length), plus music and speech variants. API: `laion_clap.CLAP_Module(enable_fusion=False)`, `load_ckpt()`, `get_audio_embedding_from_filelist(x=[...])`, `get_text_embedding([...])`. — [LAION-AI/CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP). The HF Transformers port `laion/clap-htsat-fused` is labelled **Apache-2.0**. — [HF card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/laion/clap-htsat-fused)
- **madmom**: code is BSD, but **pretrained models are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. — [madmom on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/madmom/0.14/); [madmom paper, arXiv 1605.07008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07008)
- **Essentia** is licensed under **AGPL** (Affero GPL). — [Essentia paper record](https://hdl.handle.net/10230/32252)
- **beat_this (CPJKU)**: code and weights reported as **MIT** by a secondary GGUF-conversion page, which also claims madmom's DBN post-processing is patented and licensed for non-commercial use only. Both claims are single-source. — [cstr/beat-this-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cstr/beat-this-GGUF)

### Inferences
- **Reference-SFX "learning" recipe:**
  1. `audio-separator` (BS-RoFormer) splits the audio into voice and bed.
  2. Optionally run HT-Demucs on the bed to split music (bass/drums/other) from the residual. The SFX mostly land in "other" or the residual.
  3. `librosa.onset.onset_detect` (or spectral-flux peaks) on the SFX residual gives SFX hit times.
  4. For each hit, take a 0.5-2 s window. Tag it with PANNs SED for AudioSet class probabilities and embed it with CLAP (`630k-audioset-best`). Zero-shot classify with CLAP text prompts ("whoosh transition", "cartoon pop", "camera shutter", "notification ding", "riser", "bass drop", "record scratch").
  5. Correlate hit times with cut times and caption-word times to learn rules such as "whoosh 80 ms before cut" or "pop on emphasized word".
  6. Get the music tempo and downbeats (beat_this or librosa.beat) for beat-snapped cuts.
- Mid/side analysis (M=(L+R)/2, S=(L−R)/2) is a cheap pre-step. Many reel SFX and music beds are wide-stereo while voice is centred, so the Side channel often isolates SFX/music without any ML. This is untested on a corpus.
- For a commercial product, avoid madmom pretrained models and Essentia (AGPL) unless licensed. librosa + beat_this + PANNs + CLAP form a permissive stack (librosa licence unverified, believed ISC).

### Gaps
- BEATs (Microsoft), AST, YAMNet and Spleeter licences/accuracy were not verified this session. Believed: BEATs MIT (microsoft/unilm), AST BSD-3, YAMNet Apache-2.0, Spleeter MIT. All unverified.
- MDX23C and the ZFTurbo Music-Source-Separation-Training repo were not checked. No benchmark exists for separating *SFX* (as opposed to music stems) from speech. The DnR ("Divide and Remaster") cinematic separation task is the relevant one but was not researched.
- No published "learn SFX placement from reference reels" system was found.

## 4. SFX sourcing (libraries/APIs) and generation (text-to-SFX, video-to-audio), plus CLAP matching

### Takeaway
For automated commercial use, the cleanest sources are: Freesound filtered to **CC0** (with an attribution list for CC-BY), Pixabay sounds (Pixabay Content License, no attribution, no standalone resale), Sonniss GDC bundles (royalty-free, no attribution; 2024+ terms forbid AI training), Zapsplat (free tier needs credit, premium doesn't), and the **Epidemic Sound Partner API** (paid partnership, sublicensing allowed). BBC's free RemArc archive is **non-commercial only**. For generation, ElevenLabs SFX v2 is the easiest API (0.5-30 s clips). The open models are mostly **non-commercial weights** (AudioGen, MMAudio). The exceptions are Stable Audio Open, which is free only under $1M annual revenue, and HunyuanVideo-Foley (Tencent community licence with territory carve-outs).

### Cited Findings
**Libraries / APIs**
- **Freesound**: licences are per sound (CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-NC). A licence filter can restrict search to CC0, and the built-in "Attribution list" helps credit CC-BY sounds. CC-BY-NC is unusable commercially. — [Cinevva guide 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-sound-effects-music); [SFX Engine guide](https://sfxengine.com/blog/free-sound-effects-download)
- **Pixabay sounds**: Pixabay Content License, described as CC0-like: no attribution, commercial use allowed including ads and monetised videos, but **no resale as standalone files**. — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-sound-effects-music)
- **Zapsplat** (Standard License updated Sept 2025): the free Basic tier allows personal, commercial and broadcast use but **requires crediting "ZapSplat"** and is mp3-only with download limits. **Premium** removes attribution, adds WAV, and sounds downloaded while subscribed stay attribution-free for life. Third-party descriptions conflict on plan names and prices. — [Zapsplat License Agreement](https://www.zapsplat.com/license-agreement)
- **Sonniss GDC bundles**: royalty-free, commercial, no attribution, unlimited projects (2018 terms). The **2024 licence adds "cannot be used to train AI models"** and prohibits claiming the files as your own. — [GameFromScratch on GDC 2024](https://gamefromscratch.com/sonniss-27-5gb-sound-effect-giveaway-at-gdc-2024/); [2018 bundle thread](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/30gb-of-high-quality-royalty-free-sound-effects-the-sonniss-gameaudiogdc-bundle-2018/63406)
- **Epidemic Sound API**: a self-serve free tier gives an instant key with full catalogue access for prototyping. Production requires a **paid tier / partnership agreement**. The licence is "all-inclusive, all-platform", and **sublicensing to end users** is supported. Features include natural-language search, prompt-based auto-soundtracking, search-by-video-frame, and similar-track search from a reference or Spotify link. The catalogue is 40,000+ tracks and 90,000+ SFX (developer docs), while marketing pages cite 55k tracks and 250k SFX. A June 2026 partnership with Veel (UGC platform) was announced. — [Epidemic Sound API blog](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/epidemic-sound-api/); [Free API tier](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/free-api/); [Partner API docs](https://developers.epidemicsound.com/docs/); [TMCnet, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.tmcnet.com/usubmit/2026/06/22/10403461.htm)
- **BBC Sound Effects (RemArc licence)**: free for personal, educational or research use. **Commercial use (monetised or ad-funded) requires a licence from Pro Sound Effects**, which licenses the BBC library (about 29k sounds) commercially. "Research" does not include AI training. — [Freesound forum quoting BBC guidance](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/45079); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/bbc-gives-away-16000-sound-effects-for-free/)

**Text-to-SFX / video-to-audio generators**
- **ElevenLabs Sound Effects (v2, `eleven_text_to_sound_v2`)**: the official help centre says the API costs **11 credits per second when duration is set (max 30 s)**, or a flat **100 credits** per generation when unset. Website use costs 40 credits/s. — [ElevenLabs help centre (via search snippet)](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/content-production/sound-effects/how-much-does-it-cost-to-generate-sound-effects). Third-party: about **$0.12 per minute** of output ($0.002/s), with clip length 0.5-30 s. — [ModelsLab comparison](https://modelslab.com/elevenlabs-sound-effects-api-alternative). One pipeline guide says about 40 credits/s ([xil-pipeline docs](https://xil-pipeline.readthedocs.io/en/latest/sfx-reuse-guide/)). The sources conflict.
- **Stable Audio Open 1.0**: Stability AI Community License. Free for research and non-commercial use, and for **commercial use by entities with <$1M annual revenue** (total revenue, not only revenue from the model). Above that, an enterprise licence is required. — [Stability AI licence page](https://stability.ai/license); [Stability licence update](https://stability.ai/news/license-update); [HF card describing SAO terms](https://huggingface.co/RoyalCities/Vocal_Textures_Main/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
- **AudioGen / MusicGen (AudioCraft)**: code is MIT, and **model weights are CC-BY-NC 4.0**. — [facebookresearch/audiocraft](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft)
- **MMAudio** (video-to-audio and text-to-audio): code is MIT, and **checkpoints are CC-BY-NC 4.0**. It needs about 6 GB VRAM (fp16) and defaults to 8 s output. `python demo.py --duration=8 --video=x.mp4 --prompt "..."`; omit `--video` for text-to-audio. — [hkchengrex/MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio)
- **HunyuanVideo-Foley**: Tencent Hunyuan Community License Agreement ([NOTICE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/HunyuanVideo-Foley/blob/main/NOTICE)). A secondary source says commercial use is allowed but **not licensed in the EU, UK or South Korea**. — [Creative AI News 2026](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/how-to-add-sound-effects-to-ai-video-2026/)

**Matching a reference SFX to a library sound**
- LAION-CLAP exposes `get_audio_embedding_from_filelist` and `get_text_embedding`, so reference clips and library files can be embedded into a shared space and matched by cosine similarity. `630k-audioset-best.pt` suits short (<10 s) general audio. — [LAION-AI/CLAP](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP)
- Epidemic Sound's API natively offers "find similar" from a reference track and search-by-video-frame. — [Epidemic Sound API blog](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/epidemic-sound-api/)

### Inferences
- **Practical SFX layer:** build a local, licence-clean SFX bank of a few thousand files (Freesound CC0 + Pixabay + Sonniss GDC + Zapsplat Premium). Pre-compute CLAP embeddings into a FAISS index, keeping a licence/attribution field per file. At edit time, embed each detected reference SFX (Section 3) and retrieve the top-k nearest. Fall back to ElevenLabs SFX v2 generation (about $0.002-0.01 per short clip) when similarity is low.
- Keep per-file licence metadata. CC-BY needs credits in the post description, and Sonniss 2024+ forbids using those files to train or fine-tune any model, including a CLAP fine-tune.
- For a SaaS above $1M revenue, Stable Audio Open needs an enterprise licence. For any commercial use, AudioGen, MMAudio and MusicGen weights are off-limits as-is.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Freesound APIv2 mechanics (token vs OAuth2 for original-file download, rate limits), Pixabay audio API availability, Artlist API/licence, AudioLDM2 and Tango weight licences (believed CC-BY-NC-SA / non-commercial), Kling/Veo native video-to-audio pricing, and ElevenLabs SFX licence terms for free-tier outputs.
- The ElevenLabs credit-to-dollar conversion depends on plan; the official API pricing page could not be fetched.

## 5. Music: royalty-free sources and AI music generation (licensing, platform risk, 2026)

### Takeaway
Safest for automated commercial reels: a licensed library with an API and sublicensing (Epidemic Sound Partner API), or platform-native libraries. Among open generators, **ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT code, 50+ lyric languages, <4-6 GB VRAM, up to 10 min)** is the most permissive-looking option, but its weight licence and Bengali lyric support are unverified. MusicGen weights are CC-BY-NC. YuE (now "YuE2") weights are CC BY-NC 4.0 with a creator permission that lets individuals monetise outputs but requires companies to get a commercial licence. Suno and Udio are in a licensing transition in 2026 (label deals, model deprecations, walled-garden downloads), and no public API was confirmed, so don't build a pipeline on them.

### Cited Findings
- **Suno**: in **Nov 2025** WMG settled and partnered with Suno. Suno is to launch **new licensed models in 2026 and deprecate current ones**, downloads will require a paid account, and paid users reportedly face monthly download caps. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno-strikes-first-of-its-kind-deal-with-ai-song-generator/); [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/what-suno-udio-licensing-deals-mean-future-ai-music/)
- **Udio**: UMG settled and licensed Udio in **Oct 2025** for a 2026 platform trained on licensed music, built as a "walled garden" (stream and customise in-platform, restricted export). WMG also settled with Udio, and Merlin and Kobalt deals followed in Jan and Apr 2026. — [Music Business Worldwide (WMG–Udio)](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-settles-udio-lawsuit-strikes-licensing-deal-with-ai-platform/); [Chartlex tracker](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/business/ai-music-licensing-deals-tracker-2026)
- As of mid-2026, Sony and UMG were still litigating against Suno (D. Mass.), and Sony against Udio (S.D.N.Y.). Sources disagree on schedule dates. — [AI Vortex case tracker](https://www.aivortex.io/legal/ai-case-law/suno-udio-music-ai/)
- **MusicGen** (AudioCraft): weights are **CC-BY-NC 4.0**, so no commercial use. — [audiocraft README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audiocraft)
- **YuE / YuE2** (HKUST, M-A-P): code is **Apache-2.0**, and weights are **CC BY-NC 4.0 + additional creator permission**. "Personal users, creators, and musicians" may "monetize generated outputs, with no fees or royalties", while **"Commercial use by companies: contact us"**. It needs about 24 GB VRAM (BF16). Attribution (#YuE2) is encouraged but optional. — [multimodal-art-projection/YuE](https://github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE)
- **ACE-Step 1.5**: code is **MIT** (the README does not state a separate weight licence). It claims "50+ languages" for lyrics, but Bengali is not listed explicitly. It claims <4 GB VRAM (2B turbo ≤6 GB with INT8 + offload), while the XL 4B models (Apr 2026) need ≥12-20 GB. It generates in under 2 s per song on A100 and under 10 s on RTX 3090, for durations of 10 s to 10 min. The disclaimer warns of possible "unintentional copyright infringement due to stylistic similarity". — [ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)
- **Stable Audio (hosted)**: plans include a Pro tier around $11.99-12/month with commercial use. One tracker reports the top plan rose from $89.99 to **$199/month on 24 Aug 2026**. The API is reportedly **about $0.20 per output file** (aggregator; unverified). — [stableaudio.com/pricing](https://stableaudio.com/pricing); [Costbench changelog](https://costbench.com/changelog/stable-audio-price-increase-2026-08/); [Costbench Stable Audio](https://costbench.com/software/ai-music-generators/stable-audio/)
- **Epidemic Sound API**: licensed music + SFX with sublicensing and prompt- or video-based auto-soundtracking (see Section 4). — [Epidemic Sound API blog](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/epidemic-sound-api/)

### Inferences
- For Bengali reels, background music is usually instrumental, so Bengali lyric support matters less than licence clarity. ACE-Step instrumental mode is the most attractive self-hosted generator, pending a check of its HF weight licence. The Epidemic API is the lowest-risk paid route.
- Platform risk: Content ID and TikTok/Meta audio fingerprinting can flag (a) copyrighted songs, (b) library tracks registered to Content ID even when licensed, and (c) AI tracks that closely resemble existing works. Keep licence certificates or track IDs per render, and avoid generator outputs that imitate named artists.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether Suno/Udio's 2026 licensed models have launched or offer an official API, ElevenLabs Eleven Music API pricing and licence, Stable Audio 2.5 API official pricing, ACE-Step weight licence on HF, and Bengali lyric quality for any generator.
- No primary source was found on how Instagram/TikTok fingerprinting treats AI-generated or library music in 2026.

## 6. Loudness targets, normalisation, ducking and voice cleanup

### Takeaway
No platform publishes an official Reels/TikTok loudness spec. The most-cited safe master for all three platforms is **-14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP true-peak ceiling**. Some creators push Reels/TikTok to about -12/-13 LUFS for punch. YouTube turns down content louder than about -14 LUFS but does not turn up quieter content. Implement with ffmpeg two-pass `loudnorm` (or pyloudnorm) after sidechain ducking (`sidechaincompress`) of music under voice. Use DeepFilterNet (permissive, 48 kHz, about 40 ms latency) or Resemble Enhance (MIT) for denoise and enhancement.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube / Shorts: -14 LUFS** is consistently cited. YouTube turns down videos louder than -14 LUFS and does not boost quieter ones (as of 2026, per guide). — [YouTube loudness guide](https://dj-master-web.vercel.app/en/blog/youtube-loudness-standard-en); [Opus Clip normalizer roundup](https://opus.pro/blog/best-loudness-normalizers)
- **Reels / TikTok: disputed.** Some sources say -14 LUFS is "the safe play", with creators pushing to -10/-12 ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/arthurvalle1_a2586dd2b4bc/mastering-for-reels-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-the-lufs-14-standard-2ck5)). Others recommend about -13 LUFS (-12.5 to -13.5) for TikTok/Reels ([Boya mic guide](https://www.boyamic.com/blogs/wireless-mic-gain-for-youtube-tiktok)), or -10 to -12 LUFS for Instagram ([ffmpeg viral-platform skill spec](https://www.skills.lc/JosiahSiegel/claude-plugin-marketplace/josiahsiegel-claude-plugin-marketplace-plugins-ffmpeg-master-skills-viral-video-platform-specs-skill-md)). A loudness tool notes these are "practical starting points, not official specifications". — [VloggingPro loudness tool](https://vloggingpro.com/tools/audio-loudness/)
- **True peak about -1 dBTP**: inter-sample peaks after AAC/MP3 decoding can be 2-3 dB above the sample peak, and platform re-encoding can distort hot masters. — [Pixteller audio checklist](https://pixteller.com/blog/the-audio-finishing-checklist-every-social-media-creator-needs-649); [Opus Clip](https://opus.pro/blog/best-loudness-normalizers)
- **ffmpeg two-pass loudnorm**: pass 1 measures with `print_format=json` to a null sink, and pass 2 feeds `measured_I/TP/LRA/thresh` back for linear gain. Single-pass mode can cause audible pumping. The example uses I=-16, TP=-1.5; swap in -14/-1. — [DEV: two-pass loudnorm](https://dev.to/masonwritescode/two-pass-loudness-normalization-with-ffmpeg-loudnorm-the-right-way-1nm3)
- **Ducking**: `sidechaincompress` with the voice as the sidechain key and music as the compressed input. Suggested ranges: threshold about 0.01-0.05, ratio 3-8, attack 100-300 ms, release 500-2000 ms, followed by `amix`. — [FFmpeg Cookbook: audio ducking](https://ffmpeg-cookbook.pages.dev/en/articles/audio-ducking/); [claude-video audio skill example](https://skillselion.com/skills/agricidaniel/claude-video/claude-video-audio). An FFmpeg-user thread warns that loudnorm and dynaudnorm add latency and that a badly configured sidechain can bury the voice. — [ffmpeg-user, Jul 2021](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-user/2021-July/053269.html)
- **DeepFilterNet**: Apache-2.0/MIT dual licence, full-band 48 kHz, 20 ms windows, 10 ms hop, 2-frame lookahead, so about 40 ms latency. — [LinuxLinks](https://www.linuxlinks.com/deepfilternet-low-complexity-speech-enhancement-framework/); [DeepFilterNet paper, arXiv 2305.08227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08227.pdf)
- **Resemble Enhance**: MIT. Two modules: a denoiser (separates speech from noise) and an enhancer (perceptual quality and bandwidth restoration). — [ResembleAI/resemble-enhance card (via search snippet)](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/resemble-enhance/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F3/README.md)
- **RNNoise** (xiph): small RNN denoiser. The licence is not shown in the fetched snippet. — [xiph/rnnoise](https://GitHub.com/xiph/rnnoise)

### Inferences
- **Suggested chain (ffmpeg / Python):**
  1. Voice: DeepFilterNet (or Resemble Enhance for very poor phone audio), then high-pass at about 80 Hz, light compression, and loudness to about -16 LUFS for the voice stem.
  2. Music bed at about -26 to -30 LUFS under speech, ducked by about 6-10 dB via `sidechaincompress=threshold=0.03:ratio=6:attack=150:release=800` keyed by the voice.
  3. Add SFX hits at about -18 to -22 LUFS short-term.
  4. Mix (`amix=normalize=0`), then run two-pass `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1:LRA=11` on the final mix. Pick I=-13 if you want Reels/TikTok slightly hotter.
  5. Verify with `ffmpeg -af ebur128=peak=true` or `pyloudnorm`.

  These per-stem levels are typical practice, not sourced platform specs.
- Normalise the final mix rather than stems, and do ducking before final loudnorm. This matches the caution in the ffmpeg-user thread, though no source explicitly verifies this ordering.
- Run the denoiser *before* ASR as well as before the mix. It improves both Bengali ASR (cf. Kaggle teams' noise cleaning and Demucs) and perceived quality.

### Gaps
- No official 2024-2026 loudness documentation from Meta (Instagram Reels) or TikTok was found. All Reels/TikTok targets come from creator and vendor blogs.
- pyloudnorm licence (believed MIT), RNNoise licence (believed BSD-3-Clause), and Adobe Podcast Enhance API availability, pricing and terms were not verified.
- No head-to-head quality benchmark of DeepFilterNet vs Resemble Enhance vs RNNoise vs Adobe Podcast on Bengali speech was found.
